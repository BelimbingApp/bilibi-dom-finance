# Bills, debit notes and payments

Read the book and accounts through `finance.ledger` before configuring payables. Supply an explicit company ID on every operation. The authenticated person, tenant and impersonator are never inputs. Use decimal strings in the book's minor units, not JSON numbers; rates and quantities are decimal strings too.

Every write uses the shared preview and single-use commit plan. Show the review to the person and obtain their consent before commit. Consent is not accounting approval: document and payment approval still require their own capability and the ledger's separation of duties. An override reason does not bypass same-person refusal while separation is required.

1. Set the company's liability control account with `finance.payables.settings.put`. First configuration needs `payable_account_id`. Later omissions preserve values. Set tolerances as `{kind: "percent" | "amount", value: "…"}` or clear them with `clear_quantity_tolerance` / `clear_price_tolerance`. Never set and clear the same value together. These are Payables business settings, not authority administration.
2. Create company-defined tax codes with `tax_code.create`; `tax_code.update` preserves omitted fields and cannot change the code. Inactivate with `active: false`. Existing documents retain their captured tax code, rate and account.
3. `vendor.create` accepts exactly one of `record_kind` (`company` or `person`) with `record_id`, `new_company`, or `new_person`. Core creation, the permanent Finance link and vendor relationship commit together. Core owns the fixed code; do not send a vendor code. `vendor.update` changes Finance terms, account and tax defaults, and availability. `vendor.supplier_keys` shows historical snapshots and possible collisions; `vendor.map_supplier_key` confirms one permanent match with a reason. `bill.create` takes `vendor_id` and keeps a complete draft with a company snapshot, the party's document number, document date and positive net lines against active asset/expense accounts. Due date defaults to the document date plus the vendor’s payment terms. Optional tax lines override calculated amounts only for used codes. Amounts with excess precision are refused, not rounded. Computed grouped tax rounds half up. No posting or number is produced yet.
4. `document.approve` requires an independent approver, unless duty separation is disabled with recorded evidence. `document.post` posts the approved document and its owned entry together. An open fiscal period and `finance.ledger.entries.post` are required; `finance.ledger.view` is needed for configuration, draft validation and approval context. An unposted draft or approved document can be voided with `void_reason`. A draft bill is corrected with `bill.replace_draft`: it takes `bill.create`'s fields and the draft's `document_id`, voids the draft and records the corrected draft in one step, so a refused replacement leaves the draft as it was and the supplier's invoice number may be reused. It needs the create and approve capabilities. A posted bill is corrected with `debit_note.create`, using its bill ID; the party is copied and positive lines reduce the amount owed. Its date and amount must respect the corrected bill and every later balance change. Approve and post the note in separate plans.
5. `payment.submit` freezes a request with a payment date, vendor ID, active asset/liability paid-from account and positive unique bill allocations. All allocations must retain one supplier key; confirmed historical keys are paid separately. Unmatched historical bills can name `supplier_key` instead of `vendor_id`. It does not reserve funds or post. `payment.approve` uses the request ID, rechecks dated capacity and independent approval, then posts against each bill's original control account. It returns the new payment, not the request. `payment.reject` records a reason without posting. Requests retain their single permanent decision.
6. `payment.reverse` uses the posted payment ID and a date no earlier than its payment date. It posts the exact mirror once, even if settlement accounts were since inactivated. A reversal has no allocations and cannot itself be reversed. Bills become owed again from the reversal date.

Read documents, requests and payments back with their `get` operations. Lists order by ID, oldest first, accept `after_id` and `page_size` (1–100), and return `next_after_id`; null ends paging. `document.list` can filter by `source`, `party_key`, `vendor_id`, `document_type`, `status` and `corrects_document_id`. The Bills page uses the separate bounded `document.page` projection described in the [opening-bills contract](README.md#opening-bills-at-accounting-system-conversion). `payment_request.list` and `payment.list` also accept `newest_first: true` to list the newest first: leave `after_id` out for the first page, then pass the previous `next_after_id`, which is the lowest ID seen. These cursors are record IDs, not document or request numbers. Document lines and tax snapshots are returned with their document. Corrections are not embedded as an unbounded ID collection: use `document.list` with `corrects_document_id` to page through them.

Previews include normalized values, tax amounts, exact posting lines, approval exceptions and referenced facts. Generated identifiers, numbers and timestamps exist only after commit. Payment preview describes ownership by the new payment for the request; it never reserves a payment ID. A changed review requires a fresh plan. The pinned host previews all steps before executing any; an execution-time fence rolls back the entire plan if an earlier step changes a later review. Use separate plans for dependent steps, including settings changes followed by posting, create then approve, and competing allocations. Ledger posting reviews include referenced accounts and the relevant fiscal period. Configuration, draft and payment reviews conservatively include the whole chart, so unrelated chart changes can also require another review.

Order-line matching uses Payables' own domain-event projection. Finance alone has no order source, so an `order_line_id` on a bill is refused with `names no installed order source`. When the source is mounted, posting can return `held` with exact match figures and exceptions instead of posting an entry. Review its `status` and `posting` facts: a hold consumes no accounting number. A held bill can be retried or voided; H3 adds no match-override operation. Reads expose bounded `match_context` (latest event and its approved hold, at most two), not the complete match history. An `external_order_line_id` is only an opaque retained reference. No new Payables pages are added here.
