# Receivables

All operations take `company_id`. Identity and tenant come from the credential, never input. Use the shared HTTP `/api/v1` or MCP tools to search, describe and read. Writes require a plan, the person's review and consent in the harness, then commit with the exact ordered inputs and single-use plan code. A code proves what was reviewed, not that consent occurred. Never call a write directly.

1. Open the book, create asset/income/liability accounts and open a fiscal year through `finance.ledger` first. A new book uses controlled bookkeeping; closed or missing periods refuse posting.
2. `setup.configure` selects default receivable (asset) and income accounts; both are required initially. A receivable account that receipts were already paid into is refused. Later omitted accounts remain unchanged. `setup.get` returns null before configuration; `setup.changes` reads the immutable history.
3. Create separate `series` of kind `invoice`, `credit_note` and `payment`. Code and prefix are unique within a company. Prefix defaults to empty, width to 1, active to true. Code, kind, prefix and width are permanent; update only name/active. Business numbers begin at 1 and are assigned on issue/payment, never when drafting.
4. `tax_code.create` takes an exact percentage string, at most four fractional places, and an active liability account. Rates are immutable: a different rate needs a new code. Update name/account/active only. Existing saved lines keep their tax snapshots.
5. `customer.create` takes code/name and optional flat party/address fields. Terms default to 0 days and active to true. Customer updates preserve code; issued documents keep frozen party facts. Empty strings clear optional text. Deactivation prevents new invoices, not settlement or credits of existing ones.
6. `draft.create` takes kind, series, customer and optional dates/description/lines. Credit notes additionally require an issued invoice of that customer as `credited_document_id`. Each line takes description, exact quantity and unit-price strings, optional income account and tax code. Quantity is positive, price nonnegative, both under 1E16 with at most four fractional places. Amounts and per-line taxes round half up to the book's minor units. Never send calculated totals or party snapshots.
7. `draft.update` preserves kind. Omit `lines` to keep existing snapshots; supply an array to replace all lines/taxes atomically; `[]` clears them. Empty description clears it; empty `issue_date` or `due_date` clears that optional date. Clearing due date restores terms-based derivation at issue. Drafts may be undated/empty/zero; `draft.discard` permanently keeps the record and lines without posting.
8. Review `document.issue` carefully: it shows the saved draft, current customer snapshot, derived due date, exact series reference and owned posting. Issue requires an issue date, lines and positive total. An invoice's missing due date becomes issue date + current terms. A credit note cannot predate its invoice or exceed current outstanding and uses that invoice's original control even if inactive. Issued documents are immutable; correct with a credit note, never edit/reissue.
9. `payment.record` takes a payment series/customer/date, exact amount string, active asset deposit account (never the setup's receivable account or an allocated invoice's saved one) and nonempty allocations `{document_id, amount}`. Distinct issued invoices must belong to that customer and predate or equal the payment date. Allocations must be positive, within book precision, no greater than outstanding, and sum exactly to the payment. No unapplied cash or later reallocation. Each invoice settles on its original control even after setup changes or deactivation.
10. `payment.reverse` takes payment ID, reversal date on/after the payment and a reason. It posts the owned mirror once, retains allocations and restores outstanding. Record a new payment to correct the allocation. It needs payment-post and ledger-post authority, not setup or ledger-view authority.

Setup/tax/draft writes also require ledger view; issue/payment require ledger view and ledger entries.post. Customer/series changes and draft discard need only their own capability. Reads require receivables view. Fresh grants and company writability are checked again in execution; archived companies are read-only. Source-retention refresh runs on issue, not preview; lack of source view is reported in `retention_refresh` without blocking issuance.

Previews do not reserve identities/numbers or write business/audit/retention rows. They bind normalized values, defaults, referenced records, outstanding balances and posting controls. Changed facts require a fresh plan. The pinned engine previews every step before executing any: use separate plans for dependent steps, two issues using the same series, or settlements touching the same invoice. Such a combined plan is refused and fully rolled back rather than silently changing reviewed numbers or balances. Independent steps may share a plan. Database identity gaps after a failed execution are harmless; business series numbers do not skip. Ledger entry numbers are assigned by the database, never predicted.

All amounts/rates/quantities are decimal strings and dates ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`. The schema refuses unknown/immutable fields, numeric decimal inputs and null; omit optional values or use empty text where clearing is supported. Read code lists with `page_size` (1–100, default 100) and `after_code`; ordering and cursor comparisons both use database collation. Documents/history/payments use exclusive positive `after` IDs; an empty page ends traversal. Documents filter kind/status/customer; payments filter customer. `document.get` outstanding includes all recorded events, not a historical report. `aging.get` takes `as_of` and optional `bucket_days`, needs receivables and ledger view, and returns the aged report with the posting number it was read at. Committed results contain `record` and `path`, the page of the record written (`/finance/receivables/customers/{id}`, `invoices/{id}` for drafts and issuing, `receipts/{id}` for payments) or `/finance/receivables/settings` for setup, series and tax codes. The pages call the same contracts.

## Bad debts and recoveries

Read `document.get` for the original invoice, its outstanding balance and `write_offs` history. Read `write_off.accounts` for eligible allowance or expense accounts. Propose an overdue balance with `write_off.submit`: `document_id`, `entry_date`, decimal-text `amount`, `offset_account_id`, `reason` and `evidence` reference. It posts nothing until `write_off.approve` with `write_off_id` and a review `reason`; the reviewer must be a different person, including when acting through an agent or impersonating, even if the book's general separation setting is off. Reject an unapproved proposal with `write_off.reject`, preserving its reason and evidence.

An approved write-off reduces aging and the control account together, without changing revenue or tax. For recovery, `write_off.reverse` takes `write_off_id`, `reversal_date` and `reason`, restores the full balance with a mirror entry, then `payment.record` records cash against the same invoice. Reassess any residual debt after a partial recovery. Every decision is reviewed and audited; do not construct an unrelated control-account journal or use a credit note for collection failure.

These operations do not determine SST refund eligibility, submit a claim or calculate an income-tax deduction. Keep tax review and any approved SST return adjustments separate. See the [bad-debt contract](README.md#bad-debt-journals) for permissions and limitations.
