# Receivables

All operations take `company_id`; identity and tenant come from the credential. Use `/api/v1` or MCP to search, describe and read. Writes require a plan, the person's review and consent, then the exact ordered inputs and single-use plan code. The code proves what was reviewed, not consent. Never call a write directly.

1. Through `finance.ledger`, open the book, create asset/income/liability accounts and open a fiscal year. New books use controlled bookkeeping; closed or missing periods refuse posting.
2. `setup.configure` selects default receivable (asset) and income accounts; both are initially required. Receivable accounts used for deposits are refused. Later omitted accounts stay unchanged. `setup.get` returns null before setup; `setup.changes` reads its immutable history.
3. Create `invoice`, `credit_note` and `payment` series. Code and prefix are company-unique; prefix defaults to empty, width to 1 and active to true. Only name/active may change. Numbers start at 1 and are assigned on issue/payment, never draft.
4. `tax_code.create` takes an exact percentage string, at most four fractional places, and an active liability account. Rates are immutable: a different rate needs a new code. Update name/account/active only. Existing saved lines keep their tax snapshots.
5. `customer.create` takes terms/availability and one of: `record_kind` (`company` or `person`) with `record_id`, `new_company`, or `new_person`. Core owns the code. Review Company duplicates under `changes.target.creation`; creating anyway needs `new_company.create_anyway: {candidate_ids, reason}`. Core creation, permanent Finance link and customer relationship commit together. `customer.update` changes only terms/availability. New invoices require an active customer, Core party and relationship.
6. `draft.create` takes kind, series, customer and optional dates/description/lines. Credit notes require that customer's issued invoice as `credited_document_id`. Lines take description, exact quantity/unit-price strings, optional income account and tax code. Quantity is positive and price nonnegative; both are under 1E16 with at most four decimals. Amounts and line taxes round half up to book precision. Never send totals or party snapshots.
7. `draft.update` preserves kind. Omit `lines` to keep snapshots, send an array to replace all lines/taxes atomically, or `[]` to clear them. Empty description/date text clears that value; clearing due date restores terms-based derivation at issue. Drafts may be undated, empty or zero. `draft.discard` retains the record and lines without posting.
8. `document.issue` reviews the draft, customer, frozen company facts and dated billing address, derived due date, series reference and posting. A restricted fact refuses. Issue needs a date, lines and positive total. A missing invoice due date uses current terms. A credit note cannot predate or exceed its invoice and uses that invoice's saved control, even if inactive. Issued documents are immutable; correct with a credit note.
9. `payment.record` takes series/customer/date, exact amount, an active asset deposit account and nonempty `{document_id, amount}` allocations. The deposit cannot be the current or any allocated invoice's receivable control. Distinct issued invoices must match the customer and not postdate payment. Allocations must be positive, precise, within outstanding and total the payment. There is no unapplied cash or reallocation. Each invoice uses its saved control after setup changes or deactivation.
10. `payment.reverse` takes payment ID, reversal date on/after the payment and a reason. It posts the owned mirror once, retains allocations and restores outstanding. Record a new payment to correct the allocation. It needs payment-post and ledger-post authority, not setup or ledger-view authority.

Setup/tax/draft writes require ledger view. Issue/payment also require ledger entries.post; issuing for a linked customer needs Companies and Addresses view. Customer create/link needs Companies view. Series changes and draft discard need only their own permission; reads need receivables view. Execution rechecks grants and company writability; archived companies are read-only. Issue, not preview, refreshes source retention. Missing source view appears in `retention_refresh` without blocking issue.

Previews reserve nothing and write no business, audit or retention rows. They bind normalized values, defaults, records, balances and posting controls; changed facts need a new plan. Every step is previewed before execution. Use separate plans for dependent steps, two issues on one series, or settlements on one invoice; combining them is refused and rolled back. Independent steps may share a plan. Failed database identities may skip, but business series do not. The database assigns ledger entry numbers.

Amounts, rates and quantities are decimal strings; dates are ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`. Unknown/immutable fields, numeric decimals and null are refused. Omit optional values or use supported empty text to clear. Series and tax codes use `page_size` (1–100, default 100) and `after_code`; database collation controls order and cursors. Customers, documents, history and payments use exclusive positive `after` IDs; an empty page ends traversal. Documents filter source/kind/status/customer; payments filter customer. `document.page` powers the bounded Invoices list. Unpinned `document.get` includes all events. For a dated record, send `as_of` with all three `through_entry_number`, `through_opening_batch_id` and `through_settlement_number`, or no pins. `aging.get` adds optional `bucket_days`; `control.tie_out` uses the same tuple. Both need receivables and ledger view and return their complete pins. Committed results contain `record` and page `path`; pages use these contracts too.

## Opening customer credits

Use `opening.context`, `opening.stage` and `opening.import` as described in the [opening register guide](opening-guide.md). The register may contain unpaid invoices, unapplied customer credits or both.

Use `credit.context` with `credit_document_id` and optional `search`/`after_id` to find same-customer invoices and cash accounts. `credit.apply` takes credit/target document IDs, effective date, positive decimal-string amount, unique reference and optional override reason. `credit.refund` replaces target with `cash_account_id`. `settlement.reverse` takes settlement ID, effective date, new unique reference and optional override reason. Review saved controls and `posts`: same-control application posts nothing; cross-control application, refund or entry-backed reversal posts.

## Bad debts and recoveries

Read `document.get` for the invoice, outstanding and `write_offs`; `write_off.accounts` returns eligible allowance/expense accounts, excluding receivable controls and deposit accounts. `write_off.submit` takes `document_id`, `entry_date`, decimal-text `amount`, `offset_account_id`, `reason` and `evidence`. Nothing posts until `write_off.approve` with ID and reason. The reviewer must differ, including through an agent or impersonation, even when general separation is off. `write_off.reject` retains the proposal, reason and evidence.

Approval reduces aging and control together without changing revenue or tax. For recovery, `write_off.reverse` takes ID, reversal date and reason, restores the full balance with a mirror entry, then `payment.record` records cash on that invoice. Reassess residual debt after partial recovery. Every decision is reviewed and audited; never use an unrelated control journal or credit note for collection failure.

These operations do not determine SST refund eligibility, submit a claim or calculate an income-tax deduction. Keep tax review and any approved SST return adjustments separate. See the [bad-debt contract](README.md#bad-debt-journals) for permissions and limitations.
